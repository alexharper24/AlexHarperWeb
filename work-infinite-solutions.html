Case study

Three words describing a whole company, on a homepage that never moved.

Infinite Solutions is an IT and leadership firm in New Albany, Indiana. Their previous WordPress site was not broken: it loaded, it worked on a phone, it listed the services. It had simply stopped saying anything. The entire search description was "Managed IT Services," the title was the same three ideas, and the Internet Archive's captures show an identical homepage months apart in 2024 and again in 2025. The rebuild is a hand-built static site that says who the firm helps, how, and where.

What the archive shows

A site that said too little, too long.

Every claim below comes from the Internet Archive's captures of the old site. This one is a different story from most rebuilds: nothing was visibly broken, and that is exactly why it sat still.

  1. The search description was three words. "Managed IT Services" was the entire pitch a search result or link preview could show for the whole company.
  2. One generic title. "Infinite Solutions - Managed IT Services" told a searcher in New Albany or Louisville nothing about where the firm works or who it serves.
  3. Everything lived under one page. Services as different as executive IT leadership and project delivery shared a single What We Do umbrella, so none of them could rank, be linked to, or be read on its own.
  4. The homepage did not move. Archive captures taken months apart in 2024 are byte-for-byte identical, and the same is true again across 2025. A site that never changes tells visitors and search engines the same thing.
  5. A WordPress stack for a static brochure. Eleven script tags and a CMS to patch, for pages that changed less than once a year.

The point of it

Saying the quiet part on purpose.

Most of this rebuild's value is in what a stranger can now learn in thirty seconds.

  1. Local, on purpose. The title and metadata name New Albany, because a firm serving Kentuckiana should be findable by people searching in Kentuckiana.
  2. Each service can be linked, shared, and found. A banker asking about vCIO services gets a page about vCIO services, not a scroll hunt.
  3. The homepage leads with outcomes. "How we've grown with our clients" and "We measure our success by yours" replace a list of category names.
  4. Less to maintain, less to break. No CMS, no plugin updates, no admin login to protect. The site is plain files that will render the same way in ten years.
Where it stands. The site is live at infinitesolutionsllc.com over HTTPS, with dedicated pages per service, a careers page, and search metadata that finally says what the firm does and where.
