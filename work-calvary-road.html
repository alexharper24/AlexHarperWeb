Case study

Twenty years on one platform, and a phone you couldn't navigate.

Calvary Road Baptist Church in New Albany, Indiana had been on the same church-website platform since 2006. The content was good. Everything around it was working against them. We rebuilt the site from the ground up, moved it onto a domain and hosting account the church owns outright, and carried their search history across.

What was broken

Eight things worth fixing.

The platform was built on table layouts and a 2011 version of jQuery, which left no way to fix any of this. Some problems were visible. The ones costing them most were not.

  1. No navigation on a phone. The menu was hidden below 940px, leaving the only links 3,700 pixels down in the footer.
  2. The homepage wasn't a stable address. A temporary redirect sent the root URL into a chain of ASP pages, so Google indexed a secondary URL.
  3. One page title for the whole site. Every page announced itself identically to search results.
  4. Nothing machine-readable. No structured data, no social preview tags, no canonical tags, no sitemap.
  5. Conflicting contact details. Two different phone numbers and two different email addresses across the site.
  6. Wrong service times published elsewhere. Six directories listed Sunday evening an hour off, one untouched for over six years.
  7. A whole year of events invisible. All 78 of them were locked inside a single flat image.
  8. Duplicated analytics and a boxed-in logo. Two tracking scripts loading at once, and a logo saved as a black-background JPEG.

The careful part

Moving twenty years without losing anything.

A rebuild is the easy half. The half that goes wrong is the move.

  1. Every old address still works. Ten indexed URLs were mapped to their new pages, so two decades of search history carried across.
  2. Email never went down. Both domains carried live church mailboxes. The website records were repointed and the mail records left untouched.
  3. A hidden redirect was found and removed. The new domain still had an old forwarding rule quietly sending a share of visitors back to the previous site.
  4. The church owns the keys. Domain, hosting, and the code all sit in accounts belonging to the church, with me added to maintain them.
Where it stands. The site is live at crbcindiana.com over HTTPS, with all eight pages indexed, the full year of events published in a form Google can read, and a printable calendar the office can hand out. The church can point anyone at it without a caveat.
