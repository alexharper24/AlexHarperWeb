Case study

A puppy shop on Wix, rebuilt as a directory that family breeders run themselves.

Puppy Connection in northern Indiana sold puppies from partner breeders through a Wix site built like a store, with a shopping bag in the header and every listing posted by Puppy Connection itself. The business was becoming a directory, where a family finds a puppy and talks to its breeder directly, and that needed breeder accounts and a search the old site did not have. The new site went live at the same address on November 1, 2026, and every old Wix page still leads somewhere.

What the archive shows

The site a family found before.

Every point below comes from the Internet Archive's saved copies of the old site, not from memory. The figures come from the October 16, 2026 capture of the homepage.

  1. Built as a store. The header carried a shopping bag and a site search, and the puppies page explained the business in a graphic before it showed a single puppy.
  2. A heavy first download. The homepage alone was 756,633 bytes of HTML with 81 scripts, before any photo started to load.
  3. A title that told search engines little. The homepage title was "HOME | Puppy Connection", which says nothing about puppies, breeds or where the business is.
  4. No preview for a shared link. The homepage had no social image tag, so a link pasted into Facebook or a text message showed whatever the app guessed.

Built around the family and the breeder

What the new site does.

A directory works when families can find the right puppy quickly and breeders can keep their listings current without asking anyone. Every part of the new site serves one of those two people.

  1. Search that matches how families look. One box takes a breed, a breeder, a town or a price, and the puppies page narrows by breed, price and availability.
  2. Breeders keep their own listings. Each breeder signs in with Google, posts puppies with their photos and marks them sold, so the directory stays current without a call to Puppy Connection.
  3. Families talk to the breeder. Every puppy page names the breeder raising it and connects the family with that breeder or their representative.
  4. A page for every breeder and breed. Families can read about a breeder or a breed before they reach out, and each of those pages lists the puppies that match.
  5. Every old link still lands. All 305 addresses from the Wix sitemap redirect to the matching new page, so search results and old posts kept working on launch day.
Where it stands. The site went live on November 1, 2026 at puppy-connection.com, with 313 pages in its sitemap. Breeders post and update their own puppies through the portal, and the owner reviews the directory from an admin area of its own.
