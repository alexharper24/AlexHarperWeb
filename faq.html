Pricing

Cost, plans, and what you own.

What does a website cost?

Builds come four ways, priced by how much site you need: Starter $800 for one page, Complete $1,600 for up to six pages, Signature from $2,400 with no page limit, and Advanced quoted to scope for sites that take orders, donations, or member logins. Every build includes domain and DNS setup, a working contact form, email pointed correctly, mobile-first design tested on real devices, and the setup that lets Google and AI assistants find you. A 25% deposit holds your start date.

After launch, a monthly care plan keeps the site hosted, secure, backed up, and updated: Essential is $65 a month, Standard is $150, and Premium starts at $325. Churches and nonprofits get a reduced rate on every plan. Full details are on the plans page.

Why do you publish your prices when most designers make me ask?

Most studios ask you to request a quote, which means you cannot compare anything until you have sat through a sales call. I would rather you know what things cost before we ever talk, so you can decide on your own terms. Every price, and exactly what it includes, is on the plans page.

Who owns the website when it is done?

You do. Your domain, hosting, and content are registered in your name from day one, and third-party costs like domain renewals are passed through at cost, never marked up. There is no proprietary platform underneath and nothing that locks you in.

Do I have to be on a care plan?

The Starter build is paired with a care plan, since a site at that price only works when the hosting and upkeep are handled together. Signature and Advanced builds are yours to run however you like: most clients stay on a plan because hosting, security, backups, and updates are simply handled, but if you would rather run the site yourself I will hand everything over ready to go.

Do you work with churches and nonprofits?

Yes, a large part of my work is churches and nonprofits. They get a reduced rate on every care plan: Essential $45, Standard $100, and Premium from $225 a month. Same work, same attention, same care. Just mention it when we talk.

Working together

Timelines, fit, and parting ways.

What counts as an edit, a request, or a revision round?

A revision round is one consolidated pass during a build: you review the design and send all your changes together, I make them and show you the result. Starter includes one round; Complete and Signature include two. It is not one change per round, and it is not unlimited back and forth, which is what keeps a project actually launching.

An edit (Essential plan) is a change to something already on your site: a photo, your hours, a price, a paragraph. Roughly half an hour of work or less, four a year.

A request (Standard plan) covers that plus bigger changes: a new staff member, a new menu section, a batch of photos. Up to about an hour each, four a month, no rollover.

A brand-new page, a redesign, or new functionality is none of these. That's a project, and I tell you before I start and give you a flat quote. Every definition is spelled out on the plans page.

What is not included in a build?

Writing your content from scratch, professional photography, designing a new logo or brand identity, paid advertising and ongoing SEO campaigns, and third-party subscriptions like your domain or a booking tool.

None of that is off the table, it's just quoted separately so a build price never hides it. I do shape, structure, and proof the words and photos you send, and cleaning up or recoloring a logo you already have is usually part of the build. Third-party costs always pass through at cost, never marked up.

Do you handle my Google Business Profile?

Yes, and setting it up is included in every build at no extra cost: claimed and verified, categories and services written properly, hours and contact matched to your site, your real photos uploaded, and any duplicate listings sorted out.

Ongoing management comes with Standard and Premium. Kept accurate as things change, watched for the "suggested edits" strangers can make to your listing, review replies drafted for you to approve, and a plain monthly note on how many people found you and called or asked for directions. Essential keeps the profile correct when you tell me something changed, but doesn't include the monthly watching and reporting.

What I won't do: guarantee rankings, or buy, incentivize, or write reviews. That breaks Google's policies and can get your profile penalized. The full picture, limits included, is on the local search page.

How long will my website take?

A one-page Starter launches about two weeks after your content is ready, Complete runs two to three weeks, and Signature two to four. In practice the longest part is usually gathering photos and copy, not the build itself, so you get a firm date in writing with your quote and I keep you posted as we go.

What does your guarantee cover?

Six things, in writing: anything I built that breaks gets fixed free, nothing launches without real-device testing, the written quote holds unless the scope changes, you own your domain, hosting, and content from day one, leaving takes 30 days notice and nothing more, and third-party costs pass through at cost. Read the whole thing on the guarantee page.

Do I actually need a new website?

Not always. If your current site is accurate, loads quickly, and works properly on a phone, you may not need me yet, and I will tell you so.

If you are not sure, send me your web address and I will look it over for free. You get a plain reply with what is working, what is not, and whether anything is worth fixing. No charge and no obligation.

What happens if we part ways?

Plans are billed monthly with no contract, and you can cancel anytime with 30 days notice. Because the domain, hosting, and content are already in your name, there is nothing to transfer back. I hand over any logins I manage on your behalf and you carry on.
