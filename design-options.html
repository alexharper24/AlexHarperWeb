Option B

Editorial

Depth from typography instead of shadows. Big scale contrast, hairline rules, a drop cap, figure labels, dividing lines rather than boxes. This is the visual argument for "care in every detail" made with type, and it looks nothing like the SaaS template every competitor in your market is using.

My recommendation

B for the bones, A for the warmth, plus the hero panel. Editorial typography is what separates you from a market where every single competitor looks like a SaaS landing page, and it costs nothing in assets. Option A's paper-and-shadow card treatment keeps it from going cold, which is Editorial's only real weakness. The hero panel then does the job the gold stroke could never do: it gives the page something to look at that is actually about the work.

The gold stroke stays a fine small refinement, but you were right that it cannot carry personality on its own. A 2px line is a detail; personality comes from type, contrast, and layering, which is what all four of these change.

Also worth saying: we should not borrow LWG's actual look. The thick outlines, hard offset shadows, retro slab type, and mascot are a coherent system and it is theirs, in your metro. Any client who has seen both sites would notice. Taking their structural ideas is fair; taking their aesthetic is not.
