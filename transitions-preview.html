Five ways the homepage sections can hand over

The current page mixes three separator languages: hard band cuts, hairlines, and the two ribbons, and no element ever crosses a boundary, so each section reads as a sealed slab. Each flow below is the same abbreviated homepage (photos omitted; the seams are the subject) with one consistent transition system applied. Resize for mobile behaviour. Flow D is the recommended book grammar from the design discussion. Nothing is wired into the live page.

Flow 0 · current

Hard cuts, hairlines, and ribbons

What ships today, for comparison.
Flow A

One seam language: the gold stitch

Every colour-change seam carries the same hand-drawn gold stroke the site already uses under its headings, sitting exactly on the boundary. The ribbons stay as the two structural pivots; the alt band's hard borders go. Cheapest, and it literalizes "care in every detail": the page reads as hand-stitched.
Flow B · the deep fix

Overlap and tuck

Disjointedness is really "nothing crosses a boundary," so this one makes elements straddle the seams: the three value cards ride the hero-to-navy edge, and the founder intro card rides into the navy band. Strongest sense of craft and depth; the most CSS surgery, and each straddle needs its own mobile care. Can be applied to just one or two seams on top of Flow A.
Flow C · quietest

Tonal feathering

No new elements: each navy band borrows a tall soft ramp from its neighbour's colour and the cream band melts into the paper around it. The page becomes one continuous tonal wash. Subtle at full speed, and the risk is mud: feathered navy edges can read as haze rather than intent.
Flow D · the book grammar (recommended)

Two marks, everything else is whitespace

The page as a typeset book. Paragraph breaks inside a chapter are whitespace alone: no hairlines, no ribbons, no fenced borders on the cream band. The two chapter breaks (promise into offer, offer into relationship) are a strip of page with the gold fleuron resting in it. Navy keeps its two plates and is never a divider. Every future section gets an automatic answer: chapter or paragraph.
