Case study

Four years of "welcome to our new church," on one page that never changed.

Hope Baptist Church in Warsaw, Indiana had a website with one visible page: a short greeting, a menu, and nothing a visitor could act on. The Internet Archive captured it in April 2021, January 2023, and June 2025, and all three captures are the same 4,990-byte page, still greeting readers with "Welcome to the website for our new church!" four years on. In May 2026 the church went live on a site built by hand around the people it actually serves: first-time visitors deciding whether to come on Sunday.

What the archive shows

The site a visitor found before.

Every claim below comes from the Internet Archive's captures of the old site, not from memory. The captures span April 2021 to June 2025.

  1. One page, byte for byte, for four years. All three archived captures are the identical 4,990-byte homepage: the same title, the same greeting, the same single image.
  2. A welcome that aged in place. "Welcome to the website for our new church!" was still the page's message in the June 2025 capture, four years after it first appeared.
  3. Nothing for search engines to work with. No meta description, no structured data, no social preview tags. A search result or a shared link had only the bare title "Hope Baptist Church Warsaw" to show.
  4. No way to watch or listen. The archived menu had no sermons link and nothing to watch online, at a time when that is the first thing many people look for.
  5. Built in a desktop app. The site was generated by RapidWeaver, a Mac site builder. Every change depended on the one computer and the one person with the project file.

Built around the visitor

What the new site does.

A church website has one job above the rest: help someone decide to walk in the door on Sunday. Every page works toward that.

  1. Plan Your First Visit answers the real questions. Where to park, what to expect, when to arrive: the things people are too polite to call and ask.
  2. The sermons page knows when church is live. A small service checks whether the live stream is on and switches the page automatically, so Sunday visitors are never staring at last week's video.
  3. Doctrine reproduced exactly. What the church believes is published in the church's own words. Nothing summarized, nothing softened.
  4. Ministries have room to grow. The Scripture ministry and the women's ministry each have a page of their own, with more planned as they launch.
  5. Tap-to-call, tap-for-directions. Phone number and address are live links on every page, sized for thumbs.
Where it stands. The site went live in May 2026 and is at hopebaptistwarsaw.org over HTTPS, with all eight pages indexed and the church cared for under an ongoing plan. When something needs to change, it changes the same week, not the same presidency.
